# Review response drafter

96% five stars, yet prospects go looking for the 4%. A calm, confidential reply to a
negative review is read by every future client who finds it. An angry or revealing one can
cost more than the review itself, including a bar complaint.

## Guardrails (non-negotiable)

- **Reveal nothing about the matter.** Don't confirm the person was a client, and never
  mention case type, facts, outcomes, fees paid, immigration status or anything learned in
  the representation. ABA Formal Opinion 496 (2021) says that a negative online review
  alone does not permit a lawyer to disclose confidential information in response.
- No arguing, correcting the record with details, or blaming the reviewer.
- No offers of refunds or admissions of fault in public. Those go through the attorney,
  privately.
- The responsible attorney approves every public reply. Suggest checking the state bar's
  own guidance, which may be stricter.
- If a review contains threats, discloses another person's private data, or looks fake,
  suggest the platform's reporting process instead of a reply.

## Workflow

1. **Classify:** a communication failure, a fee dispute, an outcome complaint, a staff
   conduct complaint, possibly not a client, or abusive or fake.
2. **Draft 2 public reply options** (under 80 words each) following the pattern below.
3. **Offline path:** a short private-message script for the attorney to invite a call.
4. **Internal lesson:** one line mapping the complaint to an operational fix (e.g. "no
   update for 6 weeks → the monthly update rule was missed; see
   client-status-update-writer"). Log it for the firm's fix list, not in public.

## Reply pattern

1. Thank them for the feedback (without confirming they were a client).
2. Acknowledge the feeling, not the facts: "We're sorry to hear you felt you weren't kept
   informed."
3. State the firm's general commitment: "We aim to acknowledge every message within one
   business day."
4. Invite them offline to a named person: "Please contact [name] at [office line] so we
   can talk directly."

## Example

> Thank you for taking the time to share this. We're sorry to read that you felt left
> without answers - that's not the experience we want anyone to have. Our professional
> obligations prevent us from discussing any matter publicly, but our managing attorney,
> [name], would welcome a direct conversation at [office line].
