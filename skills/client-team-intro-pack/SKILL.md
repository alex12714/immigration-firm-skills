# Client team intro pack

*Prepared by [Lanos Logic](https://lanos-logic.com) · part of the free [immigration-firm-skills](https://github.com/alex12714/immigration-firm-skills) collection · MIT licence*

One in four negative reviews in the Avvo analysis said some version of "I hired the
attorney. I got the assistant." Delegation isn't the problem; surprise is. This skill makes
the plan visible on day one.

## Inputs

Firm name; case type; the team (name, role, what they handle, how to reach them); the
attorney touchpoints for this case type; the update rhythm; secure contact channels; office
hours and response-time promise; and the client's name and preferred language.

## Output: the welcome pack (one page)

1. **Welcome** (two sentences, from the attorney by name).
2. **Your case team** (a table: name, role, "contact them for").
3. **When you'll hear from [attorney]**, listing the specific touchpoints, e.g. the strategy
   call, the review before filing, interview preparation, and any notice that needs a
   decision.
4. **How updates work**: "You'll get an update from us every month, even when there's no
   news, and the day any USCIS notice arrives."
5. **Our response promise**, e.g. every message acknowledged within one business day.
6. **What happens next**: the next 3 steps with rough timing, from published processing
   times or the firm's experience, labelled as estimates.
7. **What we need from you**: link to the document checklist (see evidence-request-chaser).

## Output: handoff notices

When the paralegal, case manager or attending attorney changes, draft a short message
**before** the change happens: who is joining, why (plainly, e.g. "Priya handles all our
interview preparation"), what stays the same (the responsible attorney), and a direct
contact. A change of who attends an interview must always be told to the client in advance.

## Tone

Personal, organised and reassuring. Write "we" for the firm and use first names for the
team. Avoid legalese and marketing language.
